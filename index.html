Before you throw

Listen to your arm.

“Whenever you throw—listen to your arm. You’ll know when you’re stretching yourself too much if you pay attention. Never throw in a way in which you lose control. Control should never leave.”

Stop if you have pain or lose control. Tell your coach or parent. Do not throw through pain.

How to use this guide

Quality before distance.

  1. Warm up with your coach. Confirm your ball weights, effort, and total throwing workload.
  2. Start with Marshall 1’s at 30 feet. Follow the sets below; each colored chip is one set.
  3. Use the 6/9 catchable-throw check with your coach. Distance is earned, not rushed.
  4. Watch the full demo, slow it down, then use “Replay key move” to see the action again.

For youth players: weighted-ball work and high-effort throwing require an individualized, supervised plan. This guide is not medical advice or a substitute for your coach’s assessment.

Coach notes: source instructions & volume clarification

The source deck says: “8–10 throws total beginning w/ Marshall 1’s at 30 ft. If using plyos split the throws between the balls with a minimum of 3 throws per ball.” The color-specific sets below reproduce its table, including 6–10 total throws for Double Crow Hop.

“If you are able to perform 6/9 throws so the catcher can receive in his stance progress to the next distance and movement. If not repeat the movement at the designated distance. After 5 rounds progress to the throwing from the stretch movement, then the full movement.”

Coach clarification needed: the deck does not define “5 rounds” or how the 6/9 check fits steps with 6–10 throws. Do not interpret that as five full circuits or add nine test throws automatically. The coach must set the number of rounds and how to score the check within the planned workload before the session.

The source permits the first four drills “whenever the Athlete desires at medium to low effort.” For this youth-facing guide, frequency, ball selection, rest, and workload remain coach-led. Ball colors do not guarantee a weight across brands. Use your coach’s chart; no weights have been assumed.

Distances, sets and reps, arm-care message, and five demonstrations come from Cameron’s supplied “Arm Strengthening / Pitching Development Progression” deck. Coaching cues are added teaching prompts, not transcribed claims from the videos. Demonstration clips are presented as supplied; some are filmed from a screen.
