Four common throwing faults: what they look like, why they matter, and the drills that clean them up.

Fault 01

Directional Side

Looks like

The front leg reaches out, or the toe opens too early.

Why it matters

The hips open too soon, so the arm does all the work and gets tired and sore.

Fix

Land with the front foot closed about 45 degrees, heel first, and let the back knee turn you. Rotate, don’t reach.

Drills

  • 45° Hookems
  • Box Squat Torques
  • Double Crow Hop

Fault 02

Elevated Distal Humerus

Elbow too high

Looks like

The elbow lifts above the shoulder.

Why it matters

It pinches the front of the shoulder and the inside of the elbow, and pitches sail high. A higher arm doesn’t make a better downhill angle. A later release out front does.

Fix

Keep the upper arm “packed” in the shoulder, like holding a kettlebell, and make easy throws with a bell club.

Drills

  • Decel and Marshall 1’s & Marshall 2’s
  • Walking Torques
  • Quick Picks
  • Jump Backs
  • Hookems

Fault 03

Forearm Flyout

Looks like

The forearm swings away from the body too early, so the arm gets long and loose.

Why it matters

It strains the elbow and the front of the shoulder, and pitches sail high.

Fix

Don’t break the hands with the thumb down and the ball pushed away. Stay tall, don’t yank the glove, and keep throws quick and loose.

Drills

  • Decel and Marshall 1’s & Marshall 2’s
  • Walking Torques
  • Quick Picks
  • Jump Backs
  • Double Plays

Fault 04

Glove Side

Looks like

The glove arm pulls or tucks on its own instead of moving with the body.

Why it matters

It stresses the arm, and it’s one of the top reasons pitchers miss the strike zone.

Fix

Firm the glove out over the front foot and turn the body to it, like grabbing a pole and pulling yourself up to it.

Drills

  • Decel and Marshall 1’s & Marshall 2’s
  • Walking Torques
  • Quick Picks
  • Jump Backs
  • Double Plays
